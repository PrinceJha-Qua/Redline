What this does and does not show

Unverifiable is not a miss in disguise. A citation without a case name, or to a page after a volume's last opinion, cannot be proved fake from the data, so Redline says so rather than guess. A lawyer still sees it flagged for checking.

Verified means the case exists at that citation. Most citations Redline verified here are real cases that the brief quoted or described falsely (the court's own words say so). Redline checks that a citation exists and what it stands on, not what the case says.

Read from the court's description, not the brief. Each citation is checked inside the database's sentence about it, which sometimes adds words before the case name. One citation is affected: "Parker v. Blauvelt Volunteer Fire Co., 93 F.3d 65" is verified here, and fabricated when checked as it appeared in the brief.
