# Benchmarks

Generated by `make bench` on 2026-11-22 from the files in `bench/results/`. Every number below comes from those files; rerun `make bench` to reproduce them.

Redline's promise is narrow: when it says **fabricated**, the citation cannot be right. So the first question is how often it calls a real citation fake, and only then how many fakes it catches.

Agents find more. Redline finds fewer, almost never flags a real citation, explains every flag with a page number, and costs nothing per check. The two are built to work together: Redline is an MCP tool an agent calls before it writes a citation.

## 5. Auditing the data before trusting it

`bench/pagemap_audit.py` compares Redline's page maps with how courts actually cite each volume. Of 149,507 pages that two or more courts cite as the start of a case, 4,056 have no case starting there in the data, and 1,895 of those fall inside another case. Without this audit Redline would call every one of those real citations fake. Those pages are protected, and volumes with several of them are not used to prove fabrications (see `docs/DATA.md`).

## What the benchmarks changed

Each fix came from reading a false alarm:

- Pages that courts cite as a case's start are never called fabricated; volumes whose page maps disagree with courts are not used as proof.
- A page shared by two opinions (last page of one, first of the next) proves nothing.
- "In re" captions, relators and co-defendants: names are compared with the full caption, not just the short name.
- A short-form name proves a fake only when it matches a different case elsewhere in the same volume ("Atkins, 536 U.S. 14": Atkins v. Virginia is 536 U.S. 304).
- Spelling and OCR variants (Muñiz/Muniz, Cornejo/Camejo, Mann/Marin), abbreviations (Nat'l, Comm'r) and acronyms (NRDC, NAACP) match.
- Words common to many case names (Fire, Bank, Smith) need a second matching word.
- Reporter publication dates are trusted only for the reporters Redline holds; reporters-db dates are wrong for some series.
- Pages after the last opinion hold order lists (U.S.) or tables of unpublished decisions (F.3d), and so may a page with no case: all are unverifiable, not fabricated.
- A case name must lead straight into its citation ("Name, 123 F.3d 456"); words like "If" or "and" are not part of it; initials ("J.J.") and docket numbers ("No. 93-1700") are read correctly.
- A page number inside a parenthetical ("109 S.Ct. 1865") is not a year.

Caveats: one model; tasks were written by us; the outcome counts only errors Redline can prove (U.S. Reports and F.3d), so arm C is scored by the tool it used. The flagged citations are listed above so anyone can check them by hand.

**A null result.** A pilot copied the design of [Precedent](https://github.com/ZNLong2203/precedent), another entry: drafting in eight famous overruled doctrines (*Chevron*, *Saucier*, *Abood*…) with a research packet of the overruled case and F.3d opinions leaning on it. A blinded judge found the brief relying on the overruled rule in 0 of 9 runs, in every arm: the model already knows these overrulings. That design cannot show a difference, so it was not scaled up.

**What the model knows without tools.** Asked whether each of the 142 overruled Supreme Court cases in the store has been overruled, it said no for 26; those are cited by 1,137 F.3d cases. Of 60 frequently cited cases no overruling names, it called 1 overruled. Details: `bench/results/agent/knowledge.json`.

Most cited among the misses: *Teague v. Lane* (1,011 F.3d cases; overruled by *Edwards v. Vannoy*); *Humphrey's Executor v. United States* (42 F.3d cases; overruled by *Trump v. Slaughter*); *Sapir v. United States* (14 F.3d cases; overruled by *Burks v. United States*); *Presser v. Illinois* (12 F.3d cases; overruled by *McDonald v. City of Chicago*); *Forman v. United States* (11 F.3d cases; overruled by *Burks v. United States*).

Some misses are arguable: *Edwards v. Vannoy* ended *Teague v. Lane*'s watershed exception, not the whole decision, and the one control the model called overruled, *Conley v. Gibson*, had its pleading standard retired by *Twombly*, which the overruling list does not record. The answers are committed so the reader can judge each one.
